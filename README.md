# EvenHold

*The old kingdom fell long ago. Its villages hold on between the forests, its
ruins lie under the mist, and below them the dead keep their crypts. In the
hills, things older than the kingdom have dug their burrows. Somebody has to
go down there.*

EvenHold is an isometric adventure in a hand-built voxel world, for adults:
wander a medieval countryside that goes on as far as you walk, hear out the
people in its villages, learn a trade, fight what lives in the wilds, and go
down into the crypts beneath the old ruins, the caves in the hills and the
bandits' strongholds.

## What awaits you

- **A living countryside.** Villages with their inns, smithies and wells,
  roads and fields between them, forests and meadows beyond. The land goes
  on as far as you walk, and every village has a name and a level: the
  farther out, the more dangerous, and the better the pay. Villagers keep
  the clock (the fields by day, the inn of an evening, abed at night), and
  any of them will stop for a word: news of a camp or a crypt nearby, word
  of work, gossip about their village. The land changes as you go: bright
  meadows, deep oak woods, purple moors, reedy marshes, rocky hills. The
  seasons turn every week, from blossom to autumn colours to snow, and the
  weather with them: rain, fog, wind, and moonlit nights. Sheep graze the
  pastures, smoke rises from the chimneys, and herons, frogs and rabbits
  keep out of your way.
- **The roads.** Roads join the villages, with signposts where they meet,
  and pedlars, pilgrims and guards walking them. Stop a pedlar to trade, or
  ask a pilgrim the way to the nearest crypt. Each village's notice board
  has a wagon to any village you've already been to, for a fare.
- **People and their stories.** A gold **!** over someone means they have
  something to ask of you. Talks play out a line at a time, with your
  answers to choose from. Begin with the carter by his broken-down wagon,
  where you set out: his errand leads to what was done in the chapel yard
  thirty years ago, and to choices that are yours to make.
- **The wilds.** Wolf packs in the woods, boars rooting in the undergrowth
  (leave them be and they'll leave you be), and bandits in their palisaded
  camps. Mossy boulders, fallen logs and old standing stones, meadows of
  wildflowers, butterflies by day and fireflies by night. Brown bears in the
  deep forest, lynxes at its edges, but none near where you start.
- **The ruins.** Crumbling keeps and chapels, lost in a mist that rolls in
  thick at night. Something still drifts between their stones.
- **The crypts.** Beneath each ruin, a crypt of its own: its passages
  guarded by the dead (swordsmen, bowmen, and worse), deeper and nastier the
  further you go. Clear enough of it and its lord wakes in the great hall.
- **The caves.** In the hills, dark mouths in rocky outcrops lead down into
  winding burrows lit only by glowing fungus and crystals. Bats flit near the
  mouth, spiders spit webs from the dark, and worms burst up out of the
  ground beneath you. Clear enough of a cave and the webs over its nest
  tear: the brood mother is waiting.
- **The bandits.** Stockaded camps, each with a name, a chief and the key to
  his chest. Far out, the bandit strongholds: a gatehouse in the rock, four
  wings each held by one of the king's captains, and the bandit king behind
  the inner gate.
- **The inn.** Sit at the bar for an ale or a hot pie, buy and sell with the
  barmaid, or call for one from a table. Talk to whoever's beside you, or
  buy a round for the bar. Rent a room for the night or the week, and wake
  well rested. Its notice board has work: serving tables, tending the bar,
  or work upstairs, paying more as you rise. A strongbox by the wall keeps
  your things, the same box at every inn. Mind the bouncer: swords stay
  sheathed in here.
- **The smithy.** Arms and armour to buy, rising with your level; the smith
  takes your old gear off your hands, and mends what you wear (freshly
  mended gear is Tempered a while). Every weapon
  fights its own way (quick daggers, parrying swords, sweeping axes,
  reaching spears, staggering hammers). Gear has a level and a rarity, and
  ten sets are scattered across the land, each among its own foes: the
  headsman's, the barrow's, the Sunward plate of kings' tombs, and more.
- **Skills.** Lumberjacking, mining, woodworking, smithing, salvaging,
  fishing, cooking and alchemy, each from 1 to 300 over a few hours: chop the trees
  and dig the ore, make what the inn and the road need, forge your own
  weapons and armour, break old gear down for its iron, catch a sturgeon,
  and roast it over a campfire of your own.
- **Quests and bounties.** The notice board in every village square has work
  for those who'll take it, and bounties on the outlaws and beasts nearby.
- **The hedge-witch.** In some villages, at the edge, a crooked cottage whose
  keeper teaches charms. More are found in scrolls, in the hoards of the
  dark.
- **The herbalist.** In every village, a green-hooded herbalist keeps shop
  in a thatched hut near the middle, a cauldron bubbling by the hearth:
  health and energy potions to buy, and they'll take your raw ingredients
  and what you bring up from the caves.
- **Greyminster.** Far out, a walled cathedral town on a hill, where the
  first village's story leads: a bishop, a false saint, his witch-finders on
  the roads, and the bishop's ossuary under the Minster.
- **A home of your own.** Buy an empty cottage in a village that knows you,
  build it up (a bed, a hearth, a cauldron, a chest, a trophy wall), and
  hang your trophies.
- **Dice at the inn.** Hazard at an inn's table for small stakes; mind the
  sharps.
- **Wells.** Toss in a silver coin and you may walk away blessed.

## Controls

| Action | Key |
| --- | --- |
| Move | **W A S D** or the arrow keys |
| Strike | **Space** |
| Roll (untouchable for a moment) | **Shift** |
| Guard (raise it as a blow lands to parry) | Hold **Q** |
| Eat or drink from the action bar (drag food and drink onto it from your bag) | **1** to **8** |
| Interact (pick up, talk, sit, open, enter) | **E** |
| Focus a foe | **Click** it, or **Tab** to the next (**Shift+Tab** back) |
| Clear the focus | **Escape** |
| Bag | **B** |
| Hero sheet | **C** |
| Spend points | **P** |
| Quest journal | **L** |
| Skills (and your charms) | **K** |
| Map | **M** |
| Dice, at an inn's table | **H** |
| At the bar: an ale, a pie | **F**, **G** |
| By the barmaid: rent a room · by its bed at night: sleep | **G** |
| Zoom | **Mouse wheel** |
| Pause | **Escape** |

Every key can be changed in the pause menu, under **Keys**.

## Staying alive

- **You don't heal on your own.** Food gives health back, drink gives energy
  back, slowly, sat down. Carry some. Potions work at once, mid-fight, but
  only one every 30 seconds.
- **Energy runs down through the day.** Below 25 you'll slow to a trudge.
  A night's sleep in a rented room puts it all right.
- **Falling has a price.** You'll come round at the last inn you visited,
  worse for wear for a while, and half your coin lies where you fell. Go
  back for it before you fall again.
- **Keep your hero safe.** Under **Your hero** in the pause menu, export
  your hero to a file or go back to a recent backup.
- **Mind your bag.** It holds 24 slots, and each bag you fit to its sockets
  adds 6 more, up to 48. Pedlars sell bags, and the odd bandit or draugr
  drops one. Junk stacks 20 to a slot. Right-click food to eat it, or a bag
  to fit it; drag anything out onto the ground to drop it. Drag food, drink
  and potions onto the action bar to have them on **1** to **8**.
- **Weigh your gear.** Hover a piece of gear in your bag or a shop and it
  tells you what wearing it instead would change: green for gains, red for
  losses, and how much better or worse it is all told.
- **Look after it.** Your weapon dulls with every blow and your armour wears
  with every hit, and a fall knocks it all about. Broken gear gives nothing
  until a smith mends it.
- **Levels bring points.** Spend them (**P**) on Strength, Agility, Stamina
  or Endurance, and gear adds its own on top. Slaying a crypt's lord or a
  cave's brood mother is worth a point of its own.
- **Read the colours.** A foe's level is coloured by how dangerous it is to
  you. A yellow health bar means it won't start a fight, but it'll finish one.
- **Some foes tell you what's coming.** Watch for marks on the ground (and
  the ground heaving, in the caves), and roll out of them (**Shift**).
- **Mind the webs.** A cave spider's spat web slows you to a crawl for a
  moment. Roll through it, or keep moving.
- **Mind your breath.** Blows, rolls and blocked blows spend it, the thin
  green bar under your health. Raise your guard (**Q**) just as a blow lands
  to parry it: the foe reels, and your next blow on it lands twice as hard.
  The guard holds only in front of you.
- **Don't just hold the attack down.** Wolves and bandits flinch at your
  blows, sturdier foes only now and then, and bears, chiefs and bosses
  only when you parry them or catch them just after a roll.

## Your heroes

Up to eight heroes, each in a world of their own. On the main menu they
stand round a campfire at dusk: pick one and they step into the light, then
**Enter World**.

Make a new one from an empty slot. Choose their body, skin, face, hair and
beard, the colour of their clothes, and try a helm on to see how their hair
sits under it; **Surprise me** if you can't decide. Name them (or roll the
dice for a name) and pick their world: leave its seed blank for one left to
chance, or type any word or number. The same seed makes the same map for
anyone, so share it and a friend can set out in your world.

The game saves itself as you play, in your browser.

## Run it yourself

You'll need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) 18 or newer.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/MysteriousSeal/EvenHold.git
cd EvenHold
npm install
npm run dev
```

Then open the address it prints (usually <http://localhost:5173>) in your
browser.

To play the optimised build instead (smaller, faster, no live reloading):

```bash
npm run build
npm run preview
```

and open <http://localhost:4173>. The build lands in `dist/`, a plain static
site you can host anywhere.

### As a desktop app

EvenHold also builds as a standalone app (with [Tauri](https://tauri.app)):
on a Mac, `EvenHold.app` and a `.dmg` to install it from. You'll need
[Rust](https://rustup.rs) once:

```bash
curl --proto '=https' --tlsv1.2 -sSf https://sh.rustup.rs | sh
npm run app
```

The app and its installer land in `src-tauri/target/release/bundle/`
(`macos/` and `dmg/`). The first build compiles for a few minutes; later
ones are quick. `npm run app:dev` opens the game in its app window while
you work on it. The app keeps its own saves, apart from your browser's.
