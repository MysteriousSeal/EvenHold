# Changelog

What's new in each version of EvenHold. The version you're playing is shown
in the bottom-left corner of the screen.

## [0.9.0] - 2026-10-15

The Bishop's Saint: the road away from home. The first village's story goes
on to a cathedral town, a false saint and the bishop's witch-finders, with a
dungeon under the Minster, a new trade, a home of your own and dice at the
inn.

### The Bishop's Saint

- **A second story, in three acts,** once "The long road" is done:
  - the carter's wagon comes home without him;
  - a hedge-witch chained to her own hearth, to free, hand in or leave;
  - **Greyminster**, the cathedral town, with Bishop Osric, Deacon Prosper,
    Sister Agnes, Captain Grey of the witch-finders and Gil the pardoner;
  - the bishop's bargain at the end: expose him, or take his gold.

  What you chose in the first village decides who vouches for you there.
- **Greyminster.** A walled town on a hill, one to a world, about as far out
  as a level-12 village. It has the tallest building in the game, the
  Minster, with a pilgrims' inn, stalls of relics, wine and cloth, and
  pilgrims on the roads who point the way.
- **The bishop's ossuary.** A dungeon under the Minster, down Sister
  Agnes's stair:
  - a nave of stacked skulls, and monks praying in the walls to mend the dead;
  - gilded saints that get up off their plinths;
  - a sealed old door that doesn't open, not yet;
  - Deacon Edric, the Gilded Deacon, who loses a reliquary at each turn of
    his fight.

  He drops the new Reliquary set.
- **Named champions.** About one dungeon in three holds one of nine named
  champions, Old Brannoc the Unburied and Long Meg of the Gibbet among
  them. Each has a trait of its own and always drops something good.

### The roads by night

- **Witch-finders.** The bishop's men in grey walk the roads in threes. Turn
  people into things and word spreads: talk of witchcraft, then wanted, then
  seized on sight. When they stop you, you can lie, bribe, run or fight.
  Caught, it's a day in the village stocks and a fine, never burning.
- **The dead walk at night.** From ten at night to dawn, a band of the dead
  rises on the road by each crypt near you.

### A life of your own

- **A cottage.** One empty cottage in every village of five houses or more,
  yours once the village knows you, for 50 silver. Build it up piece by
  piece:
  - a bed to wake Well rested;
  - a chest that opens your strongbox;
  - a hearth to cook at;
  - a cauldron to brew at;
  - a trophy wall.
- **Trophies.** Lords, the brood mother, the bandit king, the Gilded Deacon,
  champions, witch-finders and bounty targets each leave a trophy for your
  wall.
- **Alchemy, the eighth trade.** Herbs grow by country and season. Brew
  potions and salves at a cauldron, the herbalist's or your own. The
  herbalist now sells stronger brews as you rise, and brews to order from
  your herbs.
- **Dice at the inn.** Play Hazard (**H**) at an inn's table for small
  stakes. Some nights a sharp turns up with loaded dice: catch them at it
  and win back what they took.
- **The pedlar.** A mule and cart, a camp by the road at night with a fire
  to cook at, news of the road, and a rumour that marks a place on your
  map.

### Fairer and clearer

- **Every trade levels closer to its best pace** for a player who doesn't
  play it perfectly. Off-colour tries teach more often. A tree, rock or
  shore that teaches you nothing now says where one that does is, and the
  recipe star marks the recipe that teaches most for what it costs.
- **Easier to see:**
  - your hero stands out at dusk and on the first morning;
  - foes and their warnings show at night and under trees;
  - Low and Medium graphics now draw less out of doors.
- **Fixed:**
  - a quest could be impossible because a dungeon's foes dropped the same
    things every visit. Drops now vary, and a quest's item turns up sooner
    the longer you hunt for it;
  - far villages' errands raised a low-level hero too fast. Their experience
    is now capped near your level;
  - villagers wait a moment, then step past you in a doorway.

## [0.8.0] - 2026-10-15

Weight and a foothold: blows that land and a world you can hear, a map of
where you've been, a strongbox for your things, charms to learn, bounties
to hunt, and a smithy and a herbalist's worth coming back to.

### Feel

- **Sound.** The game has a voice at last:
  - steel on flesh and on armour, a parry's clang, a body falling;
  - the axe, the pick and the hammer on the anvil, the line cast, the pot
    simmering;
  - footsteps that change with the ground under you;
  - birds by day and crickets at night, rain and wind as hard as the
    weather;
  - the inn's murmur and a lute by its fire, the forge, the draught in a
    crypt.

  Volume, Effects and Ambience in the pause menu.
- **Blows that land.** Every blow that connects stops the world for a
  heartbeat, longer for a crit or a kill, and the camera kicks the way it
  went. Blood flies, lands and soaks into the ground. Skeletons shed bone,
  draugr black ichor, ghosts pale motes.
- **Greater potions are worth it.** Lesser and greater potions heal some at
  once and the rest over the next seconds, even while you're being hit:
  25%, 50% or 85% in all. Your health bar glows while one works.

### A foothold

- **A map (M).** Blank vellum until you've walked there:
  - the land, its roads and fields;
  - villages with their names, coloured by how dangerous they are to you;
  - crypts, caves and camps, struck through once cleared;
  - pins for your quests and bounties.

  A small map sits in the corner too.
- **A strongbox at every inn.** Sixty slots, the same box at every inn, and
  the chest in your rented room opens it too. Store your junk or your
  materials at a click.
- **Go back for your purse.** Fall in a fight and half your coin stays
  where you fell, under a shaft of gold light. Walk back to it and it's all
  yours again, but fall a second time first and it's gone.
- **Keep your hero safe.** Export your hero to a file and bring them back,
  or return to one of the last few automatic backups, under **Your hero**
  in the pause menu.
- **Smaller saves.** Foes that only wandered are no longer saved, so a long
  roam's save is a fraction of its old size.

### Charms and bounties

- **The hedge-witch.** In about one village in three, at its edge, a
  crooked cottage under a black, leaning thatch. Its keeper teaches the
  Hex, and the Mending charm for a bat's wing besides. She won't teach you
  to turn people into things, and she'll tell you why.
- **Charms found in the dark.** A boss's hoard may hold a scroll. Read it
  from your bag to learn the charm. The Moon-turn is found nowhere else.
  The skills window (**K**) lists the charms you know, to put back on your
  bar.
- **Bounties.** The notice board posts bounties on a named outlaw, a beast
  or a wolf pack's leader, tougher and richer the farther out they lurk.
  Bring back the grisly proof for coin.

### The smithy

- **It looks the part.** A dark, sooty room with one hot heart, the forge
  first, then the anvil, then the wares on the walls, and the trade's
  clutter round them. Outside, the forge's coals glow open to the street
  under an iron anvil sign.
- **The smith keeps hours.** He works his forge until you come to his
  counter, and goes home at night. A sleeping keeper still has a word for
  you about a quest.
- **Fairer trading:**
  - one set of wares at your level;
  - a purse that grows with his work;
  - worn gear sells for less, and what you sell keeps its wear if you buy
    it back;
  - he tells you when he's had enough of something, and what's worth
    smithing yourself.
- **Mending matters.** Freshly mended gear is **Tempered** for a while:
  blows hurt you less and your gear wears slower.
- **At the anvil and the bench.** E at the anvil or the salvage bench works
  them, never the smith. Your hero swings a hammer and sparks fly.
  Salvaging yields more as your skill grows, and pieces you made yourself
  are told apart from ones you found.

### The herbalist

- **The hut looks the part.** Peat-smoke and green glass: the cauldron's
  green glow, herbs hung thick, glinting bottles, a garden and a fairy ring
  outside, and green-grey smoke from the chimney.
- **She keeps hours** and sleeps at night, though she'll still sell you the
  pox salve if you wake her.
- **She knows you:**
  - she greets you with her part of the story;
  - she pushes a potion when you're hurt;
  - she buys spider silk, venom glands and bat wings at a better price;
  - energy potions are cheaper;
  - the pox has its own mark.
- **Fixed:**
  - a potion drunk at full health was wasted;
  - the salve's coin vanished;
  - quests ending in a night could be handed in by day.

### Easier to start, easier to see

- **The first hour teaches as you go:** short one-time tips when something
  first matters, and the carter's first errands that tell you what you need.
  Tips can be turned off.
- **A lively first morning:** the village is already up and about.
- **Graphics settings.** Low, Medium or High, and a brightness slider, in
  the pause menu.
- **Never lost from view.** Your hero, and the foes hunting you, show
  through tree crowns and roofs. Gold brackets mark you for the first
  moments of a game.
- **Rooms are framed** on their fire from the door.
- **Dungeons draw far less,** for a smoother crypt, cave and stronghold.
- **Fixed:**
  - villagers waiting forever behind you in a doorway now say so and step
    past;
  - tower shields and pavises are big;
  - long poles no longer cross your legs;
  - the rings, the dishes and a few other icons read at a glance.

## [0.7.0] - 2026-10-15

An inn worth stopping at, a countryside that changes with the seasons and
the weather, and trades that take real time to master and pay what they
should.

### The inn

- **Order from a table:** sat at one of the inn's tables, **F** or **G**
  calls the server (or the barmaid) over, and your ale or pie is brought to
  you.
- **Company at the bar:** from a stool, talk to whoever sits beside you.
  They'll give you the news, point you to work, gossip about their village,
  and for a coin tell you exactly where trouble lies. Or buy a round for the
  bar and hear them cheer.
- The barkeep greets you as you sit, and tells you your order's on its way.
  The bouncer has a word for the room now and then.
- **Work pays better as you rise:** a shift's wages and tips grow with your
  level, as quest rewards do.
- **Upstairs:**
  - keep your room for the week (seven nights for five), offered only on
    your last night;
  - or slip the barmaid a little extra for a room before four, and sleep in
    it at once;
  - a room shift can be ended at the room's own door.
- **Beds have owners:** lie in a villager's bed while they're home and
  they'll turn you out; while they're away it rests you only slowly. A night
  in a paid room leaves you **Well rested** for a few hours: slower to tire,
  quicker to catch your breath.
- **The look:** plaster walls, and warm pools of light from the hearth, the
  lanterns and a candle on every table, with shadow between. A greyed oak
  floor, leather seats, the bar's pale counter and its rail of tankards.
  Tables laid for a meal, a game of dice or a jug of wine. Barrels and
  sacks, a chalk menu, a clear way in at the door and open floor round the
  hearth. A chest, a rug and a washstand in the room you rent, a red runner
  down the hall. Outside, a big iron-framed sign with a foaming tankard and
  a lantern under it.
- **Fixed:**
  - patrons stuck at their tables after a shift;
  - an order that followed you to another stool;
  - full tankards left on the bar;
  - the server ordering from herself;
  - drinks poured for no one;
  - tips for a botched pour;
  - night shifts that got the village out of bed;
  - bar stools never served while the tables were;
  - being locked in your room at checkout;
  - clients vanishing, or frozen in place, upstairs.

### The countryside

- **Five kinds of country,** each a place of its own:
  - **meadows:** bright, with flowers in drifts;
  - **deep forests:** big dark oaks, ferns and mushrooms;
  - **moors:** purple heather and rust bracken;
  - **marshes:** reeds, flags and willows by the lakes;
  - **hills:** granite tors and boulders.
- **Seasons,** a week of game days each: blossom in spring, autumn's reds
  and golds turning tree by tree, bare branches in winter, frosty mornings,
  and snow that settles on the high ground first and melts away. Lakes stay
  open water.
- **Weather:** clear days, overcast, rain with rings on the lakes, fog and
  morning mist, wind, and snow in winter. A new game always starts on a
  clear morning.
- **The time of day shows:** a rose dawn, a warm golden hour, dusk, and a
  moonlit night you can still see your way in, the windows glowing.
- **Alive:**
  - sheep, cows, pigs and hens in the village pastures;
  - smoke from the chimneys, leaning with the wind;
  - rabbits, frogs, herons, crows and dragonflies, fish jumping, flocks of
    birds overhead;
  - deer that stop to watch you, and lie down at night.

  All of it shelters from the rain and thins out in winter.
- None of it costs the game any speed.

### Trades

- **Every trade now takes a few hours to master** (it was minutes for some):
  each level wants several tries that taught, shown as a bar in the skill's
  window. Trees, catches and rocks that can no longer teach you say so.
- **Mining (new):** the boulders and outcrops hold copper, tin, iron, silver
  and gold, with star-iron in the high country. Buy a pickaxe from the smith.
  A worked-out vein comes back after a day.
- **Smithing (new),** at the smithy's anvil: smelt ingots (bronze too),
  forge fittings, better pickaxes, weapons, armour and rings.
- **Fishing:** roach, bream, eel, grayling, char, salmon and sturgeon, and
  every kind of water always has something to learn from.
- **Lumberjacking:** the trees re-spaced, and a rare hoary elder oak with a
  burl to find.
- **Woodworking:** new pieces at every tier (a torch, bucklers, a spear,
  cudgels, war staffs, pavises, tower shields, a cask, a casket, a chest).
  Planks no longer waste your time.
- **Cooking:** a dish for every fish, up to an angler's feast. Meat dishes
  count for more, and the barmaid sells boar meat to those who don't hunt.
- **Salvaging:** no longer stalls. Junk can be broken down too, and the
  bench can break a whole stack at once.
- **Fairer money:** shops buy only a few of each made thing and sell the
  rest on over time, and prices are set so a trade earns about what
  questing does at its level.

## [0.6.0] - 2026-10-15

A world that goes on and on, people with stories to tell, bandit
strongholds, skills and trades, work at the inn, and fifty new pieces of
gear. The fighting has been rebalanced from first level to last.

### A world without end

- New worlds no longer stop at the map's edge: the land is made a stretch
  at a time around you as you travel, villages, forests, lakes and all.
  Saved worlds carry on in the world they began in.
- Every village has a name, told on a banner as you walk in, with its
  level coloured by how dangerous it is to you. Further villages are more
  dangerous and pay better.
- Roads run on from village to village across the land. Signposts stand
  where roads meet in the wilds, an arm pointing to each village. Out in
  the wilds, grass grows between the ruts; near a village, the road is
  paved.
- Every house has its own colours: painted shutters and door, its own
  limewash, chimney and yard. Thatch looks like straw, windows have two
  panes and glow whole at night, and some houses have a dormer.
- Six shapes of each kind of tree: spreading oaks, windswept pines,
  twin-stemmed birches.
- Where you set out, a carter waits by his broken-down wagon. He has
  something to ask of you.

### Bandits

- **Bandit camps** are now stockades: sharpened log walls, a gatehouse
  with its banner, a watchtower and tents. Each camp has a name ("Den of
  Thieves"), a level, three to six bandits and a **chief** who carries the
  key to its chest. Slay him to open it.
- **Bandit strongholds**, a third kind of dungeon, few and far between: a
  gatehouse in the rock, an entry hall, four wings each held by one of the
  king's captains (the houndmaster, the quartermaster, the gaoler, the
  captain), and the **bandit king** behind the inner gate. His men fight
  together: bruisers smash, crossbowmen shoot, firebrands throw fire pots,
  sawbones patch the others up, runners fetch help, and war hounds come
  at you.
- **Brown bears** in the deep forest and **lynxes** at its edges, the
  wilds' fiercest beasts. Neither comes near where you start.
- A slain beast can be butchered for its meat.

### People and their stories

- **Talks:** speaking with someone who has a quest opens a talk scene, you on
  the left and them on the right, a line at a time, with your answers to
  choose from.
- **Quests from people:** a gold **!** over whoever has work for you, and a
  **?** over whoever you report back to. Every village has its own, and so
  do the travellers on the road. Some are rare, found in only a few
  villages: the grim, the uncanny, the daft, and the ones behind closed
  doors.
- **The first village's story,** a long line of quests that begins with
  the carter's broken wagon and leads to what was done to a woman in the
  chapel yard thirty years ago, and who still has to answer for it.
- **Your first real choice:** the inn's bouncer has a secret, and what you
  do with it changes what people say to you, and what they give you.
- Quest givers hand you what you need for a job (the silver for a well,
  potions for a fight). Early quests pay what early quests should.
- **Talk to anyone:** any villager will pass the time of day. Ask them for
  news (a camp, a crypt or a cave nearby, and which way), for work, or
  about their village. Press a coin on them for the exact spot.
- Villagers keep the clock: in the fields by day, at the inn of an
  evening, abed at night.

### Skills and trades

- **The skills window (K):** each skill rises from 1 to 300 through four
  tiers, from Apprentice to Artisan. Each skill has its own window,
  WoW-style, with what it opens and what's worth doing next, coloured by
  how much it would teach you.
- **Lumberjacking:** any axe chops trees, from birches to ancient oaks, for
  logs, resin and heartwood. A felled tree leaves a stump. Walk over the
  logs it drops and **E** picks them all up at once.
- **Woodworking:** planks, tankards and bowls the inn buys, packs that make
  your bag roomier, wooden weapons and shields, a felling axe and a broad
  axe, a bedroll to rest on anywhere.
- **Salvaging:** break your old gear down at the smithy's bench for scrap
  iron, silver and stones.
- **Fishing:** a rod from a pedlar. Cast from a lake's shore, wait for the
  bite, and hook it in time: small fry, trout, perch and pike, now and then
  a huge one.
- **Cooking:** at any fire, the inn's hearth, or a campfire you kindle
  yourself. Roasts, grilled fish, stews, pies and feasts heal more than inn
  food, and leave you Well fed, Fortified or Feasted for a while.

### Work at the inn

- The notice board inside every inn has work, with ranks to climb and
  perks to earn:
  - **Serving tables:** take orders, carry them over, clear the empties.
  - **Tending the bar:** pour each drink to its line, and wash the
    tankards.
  - **Work upstairs,** for adults only: the room is yours for the
    shift, the clients come one at a time, and each hour is a game of
    keeping their pace.
- The inn fills up of an evening, until eleven.
- Rent your room for the week (seven nights for the price of five), or
  slip the barmaid a little extra for a room before four.

### Gear

- **Levels and rarity:** every piece of gear has a level and a rarity,
  from common to legendary, its name and frame in its colour and a glow
  on the ground where it lies. Gear grows stronger with its level, and you
  can wear it only from that level.
- **Fifty new pieces in ten sets:** the headsman's, the barrow's, the
  ossuary's, the flayer's, the cinder's, the palmer's, the watch's, the
  Sunward, the velvet court's and the Blackbanner. Each is found where
  it belongs, on its own foes and in their keepers' chests. Sunward plate
  lies only in kings' tombs.
- **Every weapon fights its own way:** daggers are quick and cheap on
  breath but short; swords riposte hardest after a parry; axes and staves
  sweep a second foe; spears reach a step further, straight ahead; maces
  and hammers are slow, and stagger the steady. The tooltip says which.
- **Wear and mending:** your weapon dulls with every blow, armour wears
  with every hit, and everything takes a knock when you fall. Broken gear
  gives nothing until the smith mends it, for coin.
- The smith keeps up with you: his wares rise with your level. He keeps
  his heaviest work for a hero who's proven, unless you pay him under the
  counter.
- Everything in your bag that someone will buy says what it fetches, and
  from whom.

### Fighting, rebalanced

- **Poise:** wolves and bandits flinch at every blow, sturdier foes only
  at the second in quick succession, and the heaviest (bears, chiefs,
  draugr, bosses) only when you parry them or catch them just after a
  roll. Holding down the attack no longer wins.
- **Armour** takes a share off each blow, less against stronger foes.
- Foes grow tougher faster at higher levels, so a well-geared hero still
  has to fight. Bosses can't be skipped through their phases.
- Your hero starts with 14 health, not 10.
- A parry or a critical blow lands with weight, the world holding still
  for a moment. A blow or a roll pressed a moment early is kept, not lost.
- The guard holds only in front of you, turning to face the foe you've
  focused. The roll goes forward when you're standing still.
- A crypt's lord and the brood mother are announced as you come near, and
  a cleared dungeon says so as you go in.

### Getting about

- **Wagons:** each village's notice board has a Wagons tab. Ride to any
  village you've already been to, for a fare and some hours on the road.
- **Your keys:** rebind any action from the pause menu's **Keys**.
  Your choice is remembered, and every prompt shows your keys.
- Windows come to the front as you open or click them. The bag is wider
  and lower, so it fits on the screen.

### Fixes

- Loot no longer vanishes when you reload in a dungeon. Meals in
  progress, lit campfires, potion waits and shift pay are now saved too.
- Blows no longer land through walls and palisades. A roll or a parry no
  longer gets you knocked down anyway.
- Every cave can be got into and out of again.
- Keys no longer reach a window behind the one you're using, and the game
  no longer runs on behind the pause menu.
- An ancient pine or an ancient oak never stops teaching you before an
  oak does.
- And many more, found by a full review of the game.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-10-03

Caves in the hills, something waiting at the bottom of each, and a pause
menu to match the title.

### The caves

- Out in the wilds, a cave in every stretch of the land: a craggy outcrop of
  rock at the foot of a rise, a dark mouth in its face. Walk up and press
  **E** to go in. Like the crypts, each has a name, a level (by how far it
  lies from where you started) and how much of it you've cleared.
- No two alike: a straight way in, then burrows winding down through lumpy
  chambers, dens off to the side, and a great nest at the far end. Not a
  straight wall in them.
- Dark down there. The light is the glowcaps' teal and the crystals'
  lavender, and the daylight spilling down the worn steps you came in by.
  Stalagmites, still pools, roots, old bones, cobwebs in the nooks.
- What lives there:
  - **Bats** near the mouth, in flocks, flitting about as they come. They
    bite and dart off again.
  - **Cave spiders** further in and in every den. From a little way off
    they spit webs at you: caught, you're **Webbed** and walk far slower
    for a moment. Close up they crouch and leap.
  - **Cave worms** deep down, under the ground where you can't see or strike
    them. Watch for the earth heaving under you and get off it. Then the
    worm bursts up, and it stays up a while to be fought before it digs
    down again.
- **The brood mother.** Her nest is webbed shut until you've cleared four
  fifths of the cave. Walk up to the webs to see how far you've got. Then
  the silk tears, and she waits on her silk until you step in. She spits
  webs three at a time and charges, and as she's hurt her brood hatches
  from the egg sacs round her nest.
- Slay her for a point to spend (once a cave), her hoard (gold and a fine
  piece of gear, wrapped in a silk cocoon), and a crack in the nest's wall
  opening onto the daylight, a quick way out.
- The cave's beasts drop their own spoils: spider silk, venom glands,
  chitin shards, bat wings and worm teeth, for selling.
- What you've slain stays slain, between visits and in your save.

### The pause menu

- Reworked in the gold-edged style of the main menu: your hero's portrait,
  name, level, day and world; Resume; zoom and inner walls changed right
  there; the controls; back to the main menu. It opens at once.

### Faster

- Foes finding their way round things, and you bumping into them, take far
  less work: the same game, smoother where it's crowded.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-10-02

A main menu worth arriving at, your heroes side by side, and a hero made the
way you want them.

### The main menu

- It opens on a flight over a voxel valley at dusk: clouds drifting, birds
  wheeling, forests, a lake and snowy peaks, gliding down into a heroes' camp
  as its fire flares up and the title lands. A key or a click skips it.
- Your heroes, up to eight, stand before the fire, each in their own look
  and gear. Pick one from the roster on the right (or click them in the
  world): they step forward into the light. **Enter World** takes you in.
- The roster shows each hero's portrait, level, day and world, with a slot
  for each place left. Eight heroes at most: delete one to make room (it asks
  twice).
- A village saying on a signboard under the title, a different one each
  time.
- The controls, grouped, their keys drawn as keycaps.
- No more seed in the address bar: a reload takes you back into the world
  you were playing, and **Main menu** in the pause menu takes you out.

### Making a hero

- A character creation screen: your hero stands close up before the fire;
  drag to turn them.
- On the left, their appearance: body, skin, face, hair style, hair colour,
  beard and clothes, each changing them as you choose. **Surprise me** for a
  whole new look; a helm to try on, to see how their hair sits under it.
- On the right, their name (the dice for another) and the world: a seed
  (any word or number makes the same world for anyone), or leave it blank
  for one left to chance. Type a seed of one of your worlds and its hero
  carries on there.
- More to choose from: eight skin tones, eight hair colours, eight dyes,
  two new hair styles for men (shaggy, a warrior's tail), and five faces:
  calm, cheerful, stern, wistful and sly. Villagers wear them all too.

### People

- Everyone is shaded as a rounded form, with a warm light catching their
  edges, while staying every bit as blocky.
- Close up (the menu, your hero sheet, portraits), faces and hands are drawn
  finer: eyes that catch the light, thin brows and smiles, a nose, strands
  of hair, fingers.
- Every hair style redrawn to fit its name: long hair falls to the
  shoulders, a bob frames the face, ponytails and pigtails hang against the

- Hair that hangs (long hair, braids, tails) now shows under open helms:

## [0.3.0] - 2026-10-02

Fighting with your feet and your shield, a herbalist in every village, and
a bag that's easier to live out of.

### Fighting

- **Roll (Shift):** dive a few steps the way you're going, untouchable for
  most of it. Roll out of a foe's marked ground.
- **Guard (hold Q):** cuts the blows you take: a shield more, a tower shield
  all of them. Raise it just as a blow lands to parry: the foe reels, and
  your next blow on it lands twice as hard.
- **Breath:** a thin green bar under your health. Blows, rolls and blocked
  blows spend it; it comes back a moment later. Tired, you have less of it.

### Gear

- Every helm and hood reworked in relief: raised rims, ridges, rivets,
  nose guards, brims, folds.
- Ten new helms: a kettle hat, a bascinet and a barbute at the smithy; a
  sallet on the road guards; a horned helm on bandits; and a winged helm, an
  elven circlet, a bone helm, a dragonscale helm and an old king's crown in
  the crypt lords' hoards.
- Shields reworked in relief: rims, iron bands, bosses, a raised sun; the
  buckler round.
- Hover gear to see what wearing it instead would change, WoW-style: the
  overall gain or loss in percent, then each change in green or red.
- Item tooltips laid out in parts: what it is, what it gives, the
  comparison, a word about it, its price, then how to use it.

### Food, drink and potions

- Food and drink now restore a share of your most (an apple a tenth, a roast
  leg half) over 15 seconds. You sit down to eat, your weapons put away,
  bringing it to your mouth now and then; moving, fighting or a blow stops
  the meal. A buff shows what you're eating and the time left.
- Health and energy potions, minor, lesser and greater: drunk at once, even
  mid-fight, then 30 seconds before another.
- An action bar along the bottom: drag food, drink and potions onto it from
  your bag and use them with **1** to **8**.
- Wolves now leave raw wolf meat, a cooking ingredient.

### The herbalist

- A herbalist in every village, in a thatched hermit's hut of fieldstone and
  daub near the middle, a sign, herbs drying and a cauldron outside.
- Inside, an earthen-floored workroom: a cauldron bubbling green before the
  hearth, a worktable, a drying rack, a shelf of potions, their bed, and the
  counter where they sell potions and buy your ingredients.
- The herbalist and the smith now put down their work as soon as you come
  to their counter.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-02

The roads, the life along them, and a bag that holds more.

### The world

- Roads between the villages, every village joined to at least two others.
- Travellers on the roads: pedlars with a pack of wares to trade, pilgrims
  who know where the nearest crypt lies, and guards walking in pairs. Talk to
  one with **E**. They keep to their side of the road and give way to each
  other. The wild foes leave them be, and the guards go after any foes they
  meet.
- Rocks and landmarks out in the wilds: mossy boulders, layered outcrops,
  fallen logs, cairns, old dry-stone walls, and now and then a ring of
  standing stones.
- Meadows of wildflowers: poppies, bluebells, daisies, buttercups, foxgloves,
  lavender and clover.
- Butterflies over the meadows by day, songbirds that take off as you come
  near, and fireflies at dusk and through the night.
- A foe's health bar now shows above the trees, like its name and level.

### Your hero

- You now slow down below 25 energy, whatever your Endurance. Endurance
  still gives you more energy before that.

### Your bag

- The bag holds 24 slots. Four bag sockets along its top each take a bag
  for 6 more slots, up to 48. When it's full, nothing new goes in, but more
  of something you already carry still does.
- Four bags to find: a rough sack, a leather satchel, a traveller's pack and
  a tooled bag. Pedlars sell them, and bandits and draugr sometimes drop
  them. Drag one onto a socket or right-click it to fit it. Take it off the
  same way, as long as everything still fits without it.
- Junk stacks 20 to a slot; more starts another stack. Drag a stack onto the
  ground and the item comes off that stack.
- The bag is laid out in groups, each under its own heading: Gear, Food &
  drink, Ingredients, Quest items, Bags and Junk. Free slots sit at the
  bottom. Drag items to rearrange them within their group, or press **Sort**
  to tidy everything.
- Hover an item to see it turning in a small window above its tooltip.
- What you pick up floats up over your hero, in its quality's colour and
  with its kind: "+ Wolf fang (Junk)".

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-02

The first version: the whole of the realm so far.

### The world

- A countryside made anew for every game: villages, roads, fields, forests,
  meadows and lakes, in hand-built voxels.
- Deer grazing by the woods, ducks on the ponds, cats about the villages.
- A day that passes as you play (an hour for every real minute), from
  morning through to night.
- Old ruins of keeps and chapels, lost in a mist that lies thick at night
  and at dawn, thinner by day.

### Villages

- Villagers with homes and days of their own, going between their houses,
  the square and the inn, and working the fields.
- The inn: a barmaid behind the bar who serves the regulars as well as you,
  a waitress at the tables, and a bouncer by the door. Sit at the bar for an
  ale (**F**) or a hot meat pie (**G**), or trade with her for food and drink.
- Rooms upstairs at the inn, let by the barmaid from four in the afternoon,
  for 50 copper: sleep in its bed at night and wake at eight, rested and
  whole. The other rooms stay locked.
- Weapons stay sheathed in the inn: no fighting there.
- The smithy: a blacksmith at his forge with weapons and armour to sell, and
  an eye for what you'd sell him.
- Notice boards with work to take on: foes to slay and things to bring back.
- Wells to toss a silver coin into, for a blessing.
- Benches on the village squares, to sit and watch the day go by.

### The wilds

- Wolf packs in the forests and on the meadows.
- Boars rooting in the woods, which won't start a fight but will finish one.
- Bandits in their palisaded camps.
- Ghosts drifting through the old ruins, their touch cold enough to slow
  you. They never leave their ruin.
- Foes grow tougher the further you go from where you started.

### The crypts

- A crypt beneath every ruin, each with a name of its own and a level.
- Their dead stand guard: skeleton swordsmen, skeleton bowmen, and draugr in
  rusted mail, whose frost breath and axe are worth watching for.
- A bar showing how much of a crypt you've cleared. Clear most of it and its
  lord wakes in the great hall.
- The crypt's lord: a boss with attacks of his own to learn. His fall is
  worth a point to spend (once for each crypt), a chest of his hoard, and a
  quicker way out.
- What you clear stays cleared.

### Your hero

- A new face and name every game, in a range of builds, hair and colours.
- Gear for every slot: helmets, armour, gloves, boots, rings, weapons and
  shields, all shown on you as you wear them.
- Levels, and points to spend on Strength, Agility, Stamina and Endurance.
- Health that doesn't come back on its own: food gives it back, drink gives
  back your energy, and a good night's sleep gives back both.
- Energy that runs down through the day. Run low and you'll slow down.
- Falling in battle costs you some coin, and you wake at the last inn you
  visited, weary for a while.

### Interface

- Focus a foe by clicking it, or cycle through those in sight with **Tab**.
- Your bag (**B**), hero sheet (**C**), points to spend (**P**) and quest
  journal (**L**).
- Tracked quests on screen, floating numbers for blows and coins, and the
  time of day.
- The game saves itself as you play.
